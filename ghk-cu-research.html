RESEARCH OVERVIEW

WHAT IS GHK-CU?

GHK-Cu is a naturally occurring copper-binding tripeptide that has been studied for its potential involvement in tissue remodeling, wound repair, extracellular matrix regulation, and skin biology.

BIOLOGICAL INTEREST

COPPER SIGNALING & TISSUE RESEARCH.

GHK is composed of the amino acids glycine, histidine, and lysine. The peptide has a strong affinity for copper ions, producing the copper complex commonly referred to as GHK-Cu.

Researchers have investigated this complex in several biological systems because copper participates in enzymes and signaling pathways involved in connective tissue formation, antioxidant activity, and cellular repair processes.

AREAS UNDER INVESTIGATION

01

TISSUE BIOLOGY

WOUND & TISSUE REPAIR

Experimental research has examined GHK-Cu in processes associated with tissue remodeling, cellular migration, and wound-repair signaling.

02

EXTRACELLULAR MATRIX

COLLAGEN & CONNECTIVE TISSUE

Laboratory studies have investigated potential effects on collagen synthesis, extracellular matrix organization, and other processes involved in connective tissue biology.

03

SKIN RESEARCH

SKIN REMODELING

GHK-Cu has been studied extensively in skin-related research, including investigations involving elasticity, tissue structure, appearance, and repair-associated biological pathways.

MECHANISMS UNDER INVESTIGATION

MORE THAN A COPPER CARRIER.

Scientific interest in GHK-Cu extends beyond its ability to bind copper. Experimental studies have investigated interactions with gene expression, inflammatory signaling, antioxidant systems, extracellular matrix regulation, and cellular repair pathways.

These mechanisms remain active areas of research, and findings from laboratory or animal models should not automatically be interpreted as established clinical effects in humans.

STATE OF THE EVIDENCE

01

LABORATORY

PRECLINICAL RESEARCH

A substantial portion of mechanistic research comes from laboratory, cell-culture, and animal studies examining repair-related biological processes.

02

HUMAN DATA

CLINICAL EVIDENCE

Human research exists in selected areas, particularly topical and skin-related applications, but evidence varies substantially by formulation, route, outcome, and study design.

03

INTERPRETATION

RESEARCH LIMITATIONS

Experimental findings do not establish safety or effectiveness for unapproved uses. Additional controlled human research is needed to clarify many proposed biological effects.

RESEARCH STATUS

IMPORTANT CONTEXT.

GHK-Cu remains an active subject of scientific investigation. Results can differ depending on the experimental model, formulation, concentration, route of exposure, and outcome being measured.

Information presented here is educational and intended to summarize areas of scientific research. It is not medical advice, a dosing guide, or a recommendation for human use.
