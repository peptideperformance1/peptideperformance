MOTS-C
RESEARCH.

An educational overview of a mitochondrial-derived peptide being studied for its relationship to metabolic signaling, cellular stress responses, skeletal muscle biology, and mitochondrial function.

EXPLORE RESEARCH ↓

RESEARCH OVERVIEW

WHAT IS MOTS-C?

01

MITOCHONDRIAL PEPTIDE

16 AMINO ACIDS

MOTS-C is a 16-amino-acid mitochondrial-derived peptide encoded within the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region.

02

CELL SIGNALING

MITOCHONDRIA TO NUCLEUS

During metabolic stress, research suggests MOTS-C can participate in signaling between mitochondria and the nucleus and influence expression of stress-responsive genes.

03

RESEARCH STATUS

EARLY-STAGE SCIENCE

MOTS-C has generated significant research interest, but experimental administration remains primarily a preclinical area of investigation.

MECHANISMS UNDER INVESTIGATION

METABOLISM, STRESS & ENERGY.

Research is examining how mitochondrial-derived signaling may influence cellular adaptation to metabolic stress.

AMPK

ENERGY SENSING

MOTS-C research has implicated AMPK-associated pathways, which play important roles in cellular energy sensing and metabolic adaptation.

MET

GLUCOSE METABOLISM

Animal and cellular studies have examined effects on glucose utilization, insulin sensitivity, substrate metabolism, and metabolic homeostasis.

MITO

MITOCHONDRIAL FUNCTION

Current work includes investigation of mitochondrial efficiency, cellular stress resistance, and skeletal muscle bioenergetics.

CURRENT EVIDENCE

WHAT HAS BEEN STUDIED?

A

PRECLINICAL RESEARCH

METABOLIC MODELS

Experimental studies have investigated MOTS-C in models involving obesity, insulin resistance, skeletal muscle metabolism, aging, inflammation, and cellular stress.

H

HUMAN RESEARCH

ENDOGENOUS MOTS-C

Human studies have measured naturally occurring MOTS-C in relation to exercise, metabolic states, aging, and disease-associated biomarkers.

!

IMPORTANT CONTEXT

NO ESTABLISHED THERAPY

Observing naturally occurring MOTS-C in humans is not equivalent to demonstrating that externally administered MOTS-C is safe or clinically effective.

EXERCISE BIOLOGY

MOTS-C & PHYSICAL ACTIVITY.

Exercise-related research has reported changes in circulating or tissue-associated MOTS-C and has contributed to interest in its possible role in metabolic adaptation.

These findings primarily describe biological associations and should not be interpreted as evidence that MOTS-C administration reproduces the established benefits of exercise.

RESEARCH LIMITATIONS

WHAT WE DON'T KNOW YET.

Much of the experimental evidence involving administered MOTS-C comes from cellular and animal research.

Human studies are useful for understanding endogenous mitochondrial signaling, but they do not yet establish a standardized therapeutic indication, long-term safety profile, clinical dosing framework, or proven clinical benefit from experimental MOTS-C administration.
