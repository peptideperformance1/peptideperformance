RESEARCH OVERVIEW

WHAT IS AOD-9604?

AOD-9604 is a modified peptide fragment derived from the C-terminal region of human growth hormone. It has been investigated primarily for potential effects involving fat metabolism and metabolic regulation.

BIOLOGICAL INTEREST

A GROWTH HORMONE FRAGMENT.

AOD-9604 was developed from amino acids 177–191 of human growth hormone and modified to improve stability. Researchers investigated whether metabolic effects associated with this region of the growth hormone molecule could be studied separately from the broader growth-promoting activity of intact growth hormone.

This distinction became central to research involving AOD-9604, particularly studies examining lipid metabolism and body-weight regulation.

AREAS UNDER INVESTIGATION

01

LIPID METABOLISM

FAT METABOLISM

Preclinical studies investigated whether AOD-9604 could influence pathways associated with lipolysis, lipid utilization, and fat accumulation.

02

METABOLIC RESEARCH

BODY-WEIGHT REGULATION

AOD-9604 was evaluated experimentally as a potential approach to obesity and weight-management research, leading to both animal studies and human clinical investigation.

03

ENDOCRINE RESEARCH

GH-RELATED SIGNALING

Research has examined whether the peptide fragment could produce selected metabolic activity without reproducing the full endocrine and growth-related effects associated with intact human growth hormone.

MECHANISMS UNDER INVESTIGATION

METABOLIC SIGNALING WITHOUT FULL HGH.

Early research focused on the hypothesis that the C-terminal region of growth hormone may contribute to certain metabolic effects independently of the hormone's growth-promoting actions.

Animal and laboratory experiments investigated effects involving lipid breakdown and fat accumulation. However, observations from preclinical models do not establish equivalent effects in humans.

STATE OF THE EVIDENCE

01

PRECLINICAL

ANIMAL RESEARCH

Early animal studies generated interest in the peptide by reporting changes related to adipose tissue and metabolic regulation under experimental conditions.

02

HUMAN DATA

CLINICAL TRIALS

AOD-9604 progressed into human clinical research, including studies examining obesity and weight-related outcomes. Human results did not consistently reproduce the magnitude of effects suggested by earlier experimental models.

03

INTERPRETATION

LIMITED EVIDENCE

Current evidence does not establish AOD-9604 as an approved treatment for obesity or fat loss. Proposed metabolic effects remain an area requiring careful interpretation and additional controlled research.

RESEARCH STATUS

IMPORTANT CONTEXT.

AOD-9604 provides an interesting example of how researchers have attempted to isolate specific biological functions from a larger naturally occurring hormone. Early experimental findings generated substantial scientific interest, but human research has produced more limited results.
